## Section 5 — Inheritance: 8 / 10

> Inheritance used: YES

### 5.1 Design Quality: 3 / 5

`Human`, `Zombie`, and `Boss` all derive from `Character` — genuine is-a relationships. `Character` is abstract (pure virtual `takeDamage(int)`, `heal(int)`, `attack(int)` at Character.hh:34-36). Each subclass overrides all three with distinct behaviors: `Zombie::takeDamage()` applies 80% absorption (Character.cpp:102); `Boss::takeDamage()` subtracts DEF before applying damage (Character.cpp:130); `Human::heal()` prints status before restoring HP (Character.cpp:82). The virtual dispatch chain is genuine.

Critical limitation: all three subclasses are defined inside `Character.cpp`, not in their own headers — they are invisible to any other translation unit. `Game.cpp` cannot reference `Human`, `Zombie`, or `Boss` by type. As a consequence, the entire game loop manages player and enemy state via raw integers (`playerHP_`, `playerDEF_`, local `int zombieHP`) rather than through the Character hierarchy. The polymorphism compiles correctly but is completely disconnected from the game mechanics.

The prologue packs genuine emotional setup (Rose's zombie virus inheritance, Umbrella's mercenaries, the broken family dynamic), and the ending message about domestic violence statistics (15.3% of Chinese children, 85% of perpetrators being biological parents) is thoughtful and clearly personal. This is not a game made to satisfy a rubric.

The chapter mechanics have real variety: Chapter 1 (Fear) is a pure escape puzzle — no weapons, just word-typing to stay ahead of zombies. Chapter 3 (Anger) escalates to enhanced zombies and a boss fight where the Q&A mechanic — trivia about Rose's preferences (strawberry, Twilight Sparkle, stargazing, marshmallows) seeded into the prologue text — is a genuinely clever design: players who read the story get bonus damage, those who skimmed it are punished.

The Character hierarchy is the other structural fix: `Human`, `Zombie`, and `Boss` need their own header files (e.g. `Human.hh`, `Zombie.hh`, `Boss.hh`) so that `Game.cpp` can actually use them — right now the hierarchy is real but invisible outside `Character.cpp`.
